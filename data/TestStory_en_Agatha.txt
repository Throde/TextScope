The Whispering Cradle

Protagnist: my name is agatha.

Once upon a time, in a quiet village nestled between rolling hills and dark forests, there lived an old woman named Agatha. She was known far and wide as the best babysitter anyone could ever wish for. Parents trusted her with their children, and children adored her bedtime stories, which were always filled with magic, adventure, and a touch of mystery.

One evening, a desperate couple arrived at Agatha’s doorstep. The village had been restless, whispering about eerie happenings—cradles rocking by themselves, lullabies sung with no one around, and shadows moving where they shouldn't be. But the couple had no choice; they had to leave their home for the night, and there was no one else to watch over their baby.

Agatha, unfazed by the tales, welcomed them in. She took the child, a tiny girl with golden curls, and promised to keep her safe. As the parents left, she settled into the nursery, rocking the cradle gently and humming a soft tune. The candlelight flickered, casting long, uncertain shadows across the walls.

Midnight struck. The house grew eerily silent. Then, a whisper—a faint, almost imperceptible hum—floated through the room. Agatha’s experienced eyes darted to the cradle, which had begun to rock on its own.

Instead of panicking, she leaned in closer and whispered, "Who sings with me?"

A soft giggle answered. The rocking stopped. A chill ran down Agatha’s spine, but she remained calm.

"I am Agatha, keeper of little ones. If you mean no harm, show yourself."

A gust of air, colder than the autumn breeze, swept through the room. The candle flickered wildly, and for a moment, the shape of a young woman appeared beside the cradle. She had sorrowful eyes and a ghostly presence.

"My name is Eleanor," the spirit said. "I once lived here long ago, caring for a child who was taken from me too soon. I still watch over the little ones. I never meant to frighten anyone."

Agatha softened. "You are the Whispering Cradle," she murmured, recalling the village tales.

Eleanor nodded. "I hum lullabies when babies cry. I rock their cradles when they stir at night. But I never get to hold them, never get to comfort them as I once did."

Agatha thought for a moment. "Perhaps, tonight, you can."

Eleanor’s eyes widened. "You would allow it?"

"Only if you mean well."

The spirit knelt beside the cradle, her translucent hands hovering above the baby. The child did not cry or stir but let out a soft sigh, as if comforted by an unseen presence. Eleanor wept, but they were not tears of sadness—they were of peace.

When the parents returned in the morning, they found Agatha sitting in the rocking chair, knitting peacefully. Their daughter was fast asleep, safe and sound.

"How was she?" the mother asked anxiously.

Agatha smiled. "She was watched over well. The house holds an old friend of the children, one who only wishes to soothe and protect. There is no reason to fear."

From that day on, the villagers spoke of Eleanor not as a ghost, but as a guardian. No cradle ever rocked in fear again, only in gentle comfort. And whenever a child in the village was restless at night, parents would whisper into the dark, "Eleanor, hum a tune," and, if one listened closely, a soft lullaby would fill the room.

Agatha remained the village’s most beloved babysitter, and she always smiled whenever she heard the whispering cradle in the night, knowing that no child in the village would ever sleep alone.
